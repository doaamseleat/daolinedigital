LinkedIn SEO in 2026: Make B2B Expertise Discoverable

A practical LinkedIn SEO system for keyword research, profile positioning, search-friendly posts, topic clusters, distribution, and measurable website traffic.
By Doaa Seleat • Reviewed September 30, 2026
A LinkedIn post begins in the feed, but useful public expertise can keep working as a searchable signal. The goal is not keyword stuffing. It is to use the language buyers use, explain something real, and connect that insight to a durable owned asset.
01

Choose a professional search intent

Target a problem, role, and context instead of a broad word. Customer calls, job descriptions, objections, and Search Console reveal more useful language than volume alone.

02

Clarify the entity first

Align the profile headline, About section, company description, services, and website. State who you help, the outcome, and credible proof. A vague profile disconnects a strong post from a clear expert identity.

Action: Write the reader decision before producing the asset.

03

Use a readable post structure

Open with the problem or point of view. Use short sections, an example, and an actionable conclusion. Include the core phrase naturally; hashtags cannot replace a clear explanation.

04

Build a topic cluster

Create one substantial website article, then derive a point-of-view post, carousel, expert video, answer, and newsletter edition. Each asset should stand alone while reinforcing the same subject.

05

Give people and pages different jobs

The company page holds durable proof and offers. Executives and experts add judgment and lived experience. Do not distribute an identical caption through every employee.

Reminder: One signal is not enough; combine source, behavior, and outcome.

06

Measure beyond reach

Tag outbound links, review engaged landing-page visits and key events, and track qualified messages and target-account activity. One relevant conversation can matter more than thousands of passive impressions.
